## Group A — Brief Answer Questions (10 × 2 = 20)

### Q1. Define risk, peril and hazard.

**Solution:**

- **Risk:** Uncertainty about the occurrence of a loss. In other words, it is the chance that the actual outcome will differ from the expected outcome (objective risk) or a person's doubt about the outcome (subjective risk).
- **Peril:** The *cause* of loss. Examples: fire, flood, earthquake, theft, accident, death.
- **Hazard:** A condition that *increases the frequency or severity* of loss. It has four types:
  - *Physical hazard:* a physical condition such as icy roads, faulty wiring or a defective lock.
  - *Moral hazard:* dishonesty or bad character that increases loss, e.g. faking an accident or inflating a claim.
  - *Attitudinal (morale) hazard:* carelessness or indifference to loss because insurance exists, e.g. leaving the car unlocked.
  - *Legal hazard:* features of the legal system or regulatory environment that increase loss, e.g. very large court awards.

### Q2. What do you mean by systemic risk?

**Solution:**

**Systemic risk** is the risk that the failure of one institution or market segment spreads through interconnections and causes the collapse or serious disruption of the *entire* financial system or economy. Examples are the 2008 global financial crisis (failure of Lehman Brothers) and a bank run that spreads to many banks.

Key points:

- It arises from interconnectedness and contagion ("domino effect"). It is not caused by an event that hits everyone at once.
- It is different from **systematic risk**, which is economy-wide, non-diversifiable *market* risk such as inflation, interest-rate or recession risk.
- It cannot be handled by private insurance because losses are catastrophic and correlated. It is managed through regulation, capital and solvency requirements, central bank or lender-of-last-resort support, and supervision. In Nepal this is done by Nepal Rastra Bank and the Nepal Insurance Authority.

### Q3. List out the objectives of risk management.

**Solution:**

Risk management has objectives before a loss happens and after it happens.

**Pre-loss objectives:**

1. **Economy:** prepare for potential losses in the most economical way (cost of safety programmes, premiums, etc.).
2. **Reduction of anxiety:** reduce worry and fear about possible losses.
3. **Meeting legal (externally imposed) obligations:** e.g. safety rules, compulsory third-party motor insurance, Social Security Fund contributions.

**Post-loss objectives:**

1. **Survival** of the firm.
2. **Continued operation** of the business.
3. **Stability of earnings.**
4. **Continued growth.**
5. **Social responsibility:** minimise the effect of the loss on employees, customers, suppliers and the community.

### Q4. Define risk pooling.

**Solution:**

**Risk pooling** (pooling of losses) means spreading the losses of a few people over the whole group. Each member pays a small certain amount (the premium) in place of a large uncertain loss. In this way, the average loss replaces the actual loss.

Key points:

- It is the essence of insurance.
- It is based on the **law of large numbers**. The larger the pool of independent exposures, the more accurately actual losses can be predicted, and the lower the relative risk (variation) for each member.
- It requires many similar, independent (uncorrelated) exposure units.
- Example: if 1,000 houses each pay Rs 2,000 into a pool, the Rs 20,00,000 collected can pay for the few houses that actually burn.

### Q5. Define insurance and list down its basic characteristics.

**Solution:**

**Insurance** is the pooling of fortuitous losses by transferring such risks to an insurer. In return for a premium, the insurer agrees to indemnify insureds for such losses, provide other pecuniary benefits when a loss occurs, or render services connected with the risk (Rejda).

**Basic characteristics:**

1. **Pooling of losses:** the losses of the few are spread over the many, and average loss replaces actual loss.
2. **Payment of fortuitous losses:** only losses that are unforeseen, unexpected and accidental are paid. Intentional losses are not paid.
3. **Risk transfer:** pure risk is transferred from the insured to an insurer who is financially stronger.
4. **Indemnification:** the insured is restored, partly or fully, to the financial position held before the loss.

### Q7. Define loss forecasting. List out the common techniques of loss forecasting.

**Solution:**

**Loss forecasting** means predicting the likely frequency and severity of future losses from past loss data and relevant factors. Its purpose is to choose suitable risk-control and risk-financing techniques, set retention levels and budget for losses.

**Common techniques:**

1. **Probability analysis:** assigns probabilities to events based on past data, e.g. the chance of fire in a year.
2. **Regression analysis:** relates losses to one or more variables such as payroll, number of employees, output or sales. For example, workers' compensation claims are predicted from the number of employees.
3. **Forecasting based on loss distributions:** fits past losses to a statistical distribution, such as the normal distribution, to estimate the expected loss and the range of loss.
4. **Trend analysis:** projects past losses into the future after adjusting for inflation and growth.

### Q8. How does Risk Management Information System help in risk management?

**Solution:**

A **Risk Management Information System (RMIS)** is a computerised database that stores and analyses risk management data. It holds property values, loss history, claims, policies, premiums and exposure data.

How it helps:

- It provides a **property schedule** and an **exposure database**, which help in identifying loss exposures.
- It keeps track of **losses, claims and loss reserves**. This makes loss forecasting and trend analysis easier.
- It helps in **comparing insurance options**, renewing policies and allocating insurance costs among departments.
- It supports **monitoring and reporting** to management and to regulators. In this way it improves decision-making and keeps the programme cost-effective.

### Q9. What are the tax benefits and payout taxes for life insurance in Nepal?

**Solution:**

As per current Income Tax Act, 2058 provisions (verify against the latest Finance Act):

**Tax benefits (on premium):**

- **Life insurance premium** paid by a resident individual is deductible from taxable income. The limit is the actual premium or **Rs 40,000 per year**, whichever is lower.
- **Health insurance premium** is deductible up to **Rs 20,000 per year**.
- Contributions to approved retirement funds (EPF, CIT, SSF) are also deductible within the prescribed limits.

**Payout taxes:**

- The **sum assured received on death** of the insured, received by the nominee, is generally **tax-exempt**.
- The **maturity benefit** of a life policy is generally exempt for the insured. However, the investment-type gain or return component may attract **TDS of about 5%** where applicable.
- Surrender of a policy before the prescribed period may lead to the tax-deduction benefit being withdrawn or taxed.

### Q10. Define third-party liability insurance in terms of auto (motor) insurance.

**Solution:**

**Third-party liability motor insurance** covers the vehicle owner's or driver's **legal liability to other persons (the third party)** for **death, bodily injury or property damage** caused by the use of the insured vehicle.

Key points:

- There are three parties: the insured (first party), the insurer (second party) and the injured outsider (third party).
- It is **compulsory** in Nepal under the **Motor Vehicle and Transport Management Act, 2049** and the directives of the Nepal Insurance Authority. A vehicle cannot be renewed or registered without it.
- It does **not** cover **own damage** to the insured vehicle. Own damage needs a comprehensive policy.
- The premium is fixed by the regulator according to the type and capacity of the vehicle.

### Q11. Differentiate between insurance and gambling.

**Solution:**

Insurance and gambling look alike because in both, a small amount is paid and a large amount may be received on an uncertain event. However, they are fundamentally different.

| Basis | Insurance | Gambling |
|---|---|---|
| Nature of risk | Deals with an **existing pure risk** (loss or no loss) | **Creates a new speculative risk** (loss, gain or no change) |
| Purpose | Protection against loss | Profit or gain |
| Social value | **Socially productive:** both insurer and insured gain when no loss occurs | **Socially unproductive:** the winner gains only at the loser's expense |
| Indemnity | Restores the insured to the pre-loss position; no profit allowed | No indemnity; the gambler can gain far more than the stake |
| Insurable interest | Must be present; the insured must suffer a financial loss | Not required |
| Basis of prediction | Based on the **law of large numbers** and scientific premium calculation | Based on chance; no pooling of losses |
| Effect on society | Reduces uncertainty and promotes savings, investment and credit | Increases uncertainty; may cause addiction and social problems |
| Legal status | Legal, regulated contract (by NIA in Nepal) | Often illegal or heavily restricted |

**Conclusion:** Insurance is a risk-*reducing* device that transfers and pools an existing risk. Gambling is a risk-*creating* activity. Therefore insurance is a legitimate financial service and gambling is not.

### Q12. Explain the concept and significance of law of large number in risk pooling?

**Solution:**

**Concept**

The **law of large numbers** says that as the number of exposure units (the sample) increases, the actual results get closer to the expected results. Put another way, the relative variation of actual losses from expected losses becomes smaller.

For example, if the probability of a house fire is 1%, an insurer with 10 houses cannot predict losses. An insurer with 100,000 similar houses can expect about 1,000 fires and will be fairly accurate.

**Conditions for it to work**

- There must be a large number of **homogeneous**, similar exposure units.
- Losses must be **independent** (not correlated or catastrophic).
- The loss must be **accidental** and **measurable**.

**Significance in risk pooling**

1. **Accurate prediction of losses:** the insurer can estimate the expected loss and charge a fair premium.
2. **Reduction of objective risk:** risk falls in proportion to 1/√N. As the pool grows, uncertainty for the insurer falls.
3. **Basis for premium calculation:** the expected loss per unit becomes the pure premium, and loading is added to it.
4. **Makes insurance affordable:** each member pays a small, certain premium in place of a large, uncertain loss.
5. **Financial stability of insurers:** predictable claims allow proper reserves and solvency.
6. **Explains why insurers avoid catastrophic risks:** correlated losses break the law. Examples are earthquake losses in one area, as in Nepal in 2015. Such risks need reinsurance, for example with Nepal Re-Insurance Company.

**Conclusion:** The law of large numbers is the mathematical foundation of insurance. It turns individual uncertainty into group predictability.

### Q13. Define adverse selection. Explain the tools used by insurance companies to control the chance of adverse selection.

**Solution:**

**Adverse selection** is the tendency of people with a **higher-than-average chance of loss** to seek insurance at **standard (average) rates**. If this is not controlled, actual losses become higher than expected. It arises from **asymmetric information**: the applicant knows more about their own risk than the insurer does. Examples are a person with heart disease buying life or health insurance, or a house owner in a flood zone buying flood cover.

**Tools to control adverse selection**

1. **Careful underwriting:** the insurer selects and classifies applicants. It may accept them at standard rates, accept them at higher rates, or reject them.
2. **Medical examination and proposal form:** medical tests, health questionnaires and the duty of utmost good faith reveal hidden risk factors.
3. **Risk classification and rating:** applicants are grouped by age, health, occupation and location. Higher-risk groups pay higher premiums, for example substandard rating with extra premium.
4. **Policy provisions:**
   - *Pre-existing condition exclusion* and *waiting period* in health insurance.
   - *Suicide clause:* suicide within the first 1–2 years is not covered.
   - *Incontestable or contestable period:* the insurer can contest the policy for misrepresentation within 2 years.
   - Exclusions for war, hazardous activities and similar risks.
5. **Deductibles and coinsurance:** the insured shares part of the loss. This discourages high-risk or careless buyers.
6. **Information sharing:** insurers share claim data, the motor claim history and blacklists through the regulator or industry databases.
7. **Group underwriting:** people are insured as a group (for example, employees) who joined for reasons other than buying insurance. This dilutes individual adverse selection.

### Q14. Explain about premature death. Also explain its financial implications.

**Solution:**

**Premature death** is the death of a family head with **unfulfilled financial obligations**. These include dependants to support, children to educate, and a mortgage or other debts to pay off. A death is "premature" because of these financial responsibilities, not only because of age.

**Financial implications (costs of premature death)**

1. **Loss of human life value:** the family permanently loses its share of the deceased's future earnings. This loss can be very large.
2. **Additional expenses:** funeral expenses, medical bills of the last illness, estate settlement costs and unpaid debts.
3. **Insufficient income for the family:** many families do not have enough savings or insurance. Their living standard falls, and children's education may stop.
4. **Non-economic costs:** emotional grief, loss of a role model, loss of guidance for children, and loss of household services.

**When premature death causes financial insecurity**

- When the deceased has **dependants** (spouse, children, parents).
- When there are **outstanding debts** such as a home loan or car loan.
- When there are **children to educate**.
- When a **single-parent** family loses its only earner.
- When there is **insufficient life insurance** or savings.

A person with no dependants, for example a single person, causes little financial hardship to others.

**Management:** Life insurance (term or whole life), social security benefits (dependent family benefits under the SSF in Nepal), and savings, EPF and CIT balances reduce these financial consequences.

### Q15. Mr. Suman Thapa, aged 40, is married and has two children. He earns Rs.600,000 annually and plans to retire at age 60. Of this amount, Rs.30,000 is used for taxes, life and health insurance, and his personal needs. The remaining amount is used to support his family. Using a discount rate of 6 percent, calculate the human life value of Mr. Shrestha. Note that the present value of Re.1 received annually for 20 years at 6% is Rs.11.4699.

**Solution:**

*Note: The question names "Mr. Suman Thapa" first and "Mr. Shrestha" later. Both are taken to be the same person.*

**Given:**

- Present age = 40 years; retirement age = 60 years
- Annual earnings = Rs 600,000
- Self-maintenance costs (taxes, life & health insurance, personal needs) = Rs 30,000 per year
- Discount rate (r) = 6%
- PVIFA (6%, 20 years) = 11.4699

**Formula:**

Human Life Value (HLV) = Annual amount available to family × PVIFA(r, n)

where annual amount available to family = Annual earnings − Self-maintenance costs, and n = Retirement age − Present age.

**Step-by-step calculation:**

Step 1: Working years remaining
n = 60 − 40 = **20 years**

Step 2: Annual amount used to support the family
= 600,000 − 30,000 = **Rs 570,000**

Step 3: Present value of this annual amount for 20 years at 6%
HLV = 570,000 × 11.4699
= (570,000 × 11) + (570,000 × 0.4699)
= 6,270,000 + 267,843
= **Rs 6,537,843**

**Answer:** The human life value of Mr. Suman Thapa (Mr. Shrestha) is **Rs 6,537,843** (about Rs 65.38 lakh). This is the amount of life insurance needed to replace his economic value to his family.

> 💡 **Exam Hint (remember):**
> Formula: **HLV = (Annual earnings − Self-maintenance expenses) × PVIFA(r, n)**, where n = retirement age − current age.
> Common trap: forgetting to **deduct personal expenses, taxes and insurance**. Using Rs 600,000 gives the wrong answer of Rs 6,881,940. Another trap is using retirement age (60) as n instead of the years left (20).
> Shortcut: "Family share × annuity factor": 5.7 lakh × 11.4699 ≈ 65.38 lakh.

### Q16. Explain the third-party liability insurance in terms of auto (motor) insurance.

**Solution:**

**Meaning**

Third-party liability motor insurance protects the owner or driver of a vehicle against the **legal liability** to pay compensation to a **third party** (any person other than the insured and the insurer). The liability must arise from **death, bodily injury or property damage** caused by an accident involving the insured vehicle. The insurer pays the third party on behalf of the insured.

**Parties involved**

- *First party:* the insured vehicle owner.
- *Second party:* the insurance company.
- *Third party:* the victim, such as a pedestrian, a passenger of another vehicle, or the owner of damaged property.

**Coverage**

1. **Death or bodily injury** of third parties, including medical expenses, up to the prescribed limits.
2. **Damage to third-party property**, such as another vehicle, a house or a wall, up to the prescribed limit.
3. **Legal costs** of defending claims, as provided in the policy.
4. In Nepal, the tariff usually also includes cover for the **driver, helper/conductor and passengers**.

**Exclusions**

- **Own damage** to the insured vehicle (needs a comprehensive policy).
- Driving without a valid licence, under the influence of alcohol or drugs, or for an unauthorised purpose.
- Intentional damage, war, and nuclear risks.
- Contractual liability.

**Legal position in Nepal**

- It is **compulsory** under the **Motor Vehicle and Transport Management Act, 2049**. A vehicle cannot legally run on the road, nor be renewed (bluebook), without valid third-party insurance.
- The **Nepal Insurance Authority** fixes the uniform premium tariff and the compensation limits by vehicle type and capacity.

**Importance**

- It ensures that **accident victims get compensation** even if the driver cannot pay.
- It protects vehicle owners from large, unexpected legal liabilities.
- It is cheap compared with a comprehensive policy, and it promotes social justice and road safety.

### Q17. Explain social insurance with few examples.

**Solution:**

**Meaning**

**Social insurance** is a government insurance programme with certain characteristics that set it apart from other government programmes. It provides protection against social risks such as old age, disability, death, sickness, unemployment and work injury. It is usually **compulsory** and **financed by contributions** from employers, employees and sometimes the government.

**Characteristics**

1. **Compulsory programmes:** coverage is mandatory for eligible workers.
2. **Floor of income:** it provides only a minimum, basic level of protection. Individuals add private insurance on top.
3. **Emphasis on social adequacy:** benefits favour low-income groups. This means **income redistribution**, rather than strict individual equity.
4. **Benefits loosely related to earnings:** higher contributions usually give somewhat higher benefits.
5. **Benefits prescribed by law:** they are not set by an individual contract.
6. **No needs (means) test:** benefits are paid as a right if eligibility conditions are met.
7. **Full funding not necessary:** benefits are often financed on a pay-as-you-go basis.
8. **Financed mainly by contributions:** from covered employees and employers (for example, payroll taxes).

**Examples – Nepal**

- **Social Security Fund (SSF):** a contribution-based scheme under the Contribution-based Social Security Act, 2075. It includes the medical, health and maternity scheme; the accident and disability scheme; the dependent family scheme; and the old-age protection scheme. The total contribution is 31% (11% employee + 20% employer) of basic salary.
- **Employees Provident Fund (EPF):** compulsory retirement savings for government and other employees.
- **Citizen Investment Trust (CIT):** retirement and gratuity funds.
- **Health Insurance Board scheme:** national health insurance.

**Examples – International (USA)**

- **OASDI (Old-Age, Survivors and Disability Insurance):** the Social Security programme.
- **Unemployment insurance.**
- **Workers' compensation** (for job-related injury or disease).
- **Medicare** (health insurance for the elderly).

### Q18. Explain the concept and process of risk management.

**Solution:**

**Concept of risk management**

Risk management is a **systematic process for identifying and evaluating pure loss exposures** faced by an organisation or individual, and for **selecting and implementing the most appropriate techniques** for treating those exposures (Rejda). Its aim is to protect assets, earnings and people at the lowest possible cost. The broader **Enterprise Risk Management (ERM)** approach covers pure, speculative, strategic, financial and operational risks together.

It has pre-loss objectives (economy, reduction of anxiety, meeting legal obligations) and post-loss objectives (survival, continued operation, stable earnings, continued growth and social responsibility).

**Process of risk management**

**Step 1: Identify loss exposures**

All potential losses are identified:

- *Property exposures:* buildings, equipment, inventory, vehicles.
- *Liability exposures:* defective products, environmental pollution, professional errors.
- *Business income exposures:* loss of profit due to interruption.
- *Human resource exposures:* death, disability or retirement of key employees, and work injury.
- *Crime, cyber, and foreign/political exposures.*

Sources of information include questionnaires, physical inspection, flowcharts, financial statements, past loss data and the RMIS.

**Step 2: Measure and analyse the loss exposures**

Each exposure is estimated for:

- **Loss frequency:** the probable number of losses.
- **Loss severity:** the probable size of losses, including the *maximum possible loss* and the *probable maximum loss*.

Exposures are then ranked by importance. Severity is more important than frequency because a single catastrophic loss can destroy a firm.

**Step 3: Select the appropriate combination of techniques**

*A. Risk control (reduce frequency or severity):*

- **Avoidance:** not acquiring or abandoning the exposure, e.g. not producing a risky product.
- **Loss prevention:** reducing *frequency*, e.g. safety training, maintenance.
- **Loss reduction:** reducing *severity*, e.g. sprinklers, fire alarms, rehabilitation.
- **Duplication:** keeping back-ups or spares, e.g. data backups.
- **Separation:** dividing assets among several locations.
- **Diversification:** spreading business across products, customers or markets.

*B. Risk financing (pay for losses that occur):*

- **Retention:** the firm pays losses itself, through current income, funded reserves, a credit line or a captive insurer. It is suitable for high-frequency, low-severity losses.
- **Non-insurance transfers:** contracts, leases, hold-harmless agreements, hedging, incorporation.
- **Commercial insurance:** suitable for low-frequency, high-severity losses. It involves choosing the insurer, coverage, deductibles and limits.

*Risk management matrix:*

| | Low severity | High severity |
|---|---|---|
| **Low frequency** | Retention | Insurance |
| **High frequency** | Loss prevention + retention | Avoidance / loss reduction |

**Step 4: Implement and monitor the risk management programme**

- Prepare a **risk management policy statement**.
- Coordinate with other departments (accounting, finance, HR, production).
- **Monitor and review** the programme periodically, because exposures, laws and costs change. Use risk management audits and the RMIS.

**Conclusion:** Risk management is a continuous cycle: identify, measure, treat and monitor. It lets an organisation lower the cost of risk, maintain stability and achieve its goals.

### Q19. Explain the fundamental legal principles of insurance contract.

**Solution:**

An insurance contract is a legal contract. It must meet general contract requirements and also follow certain special principles.

**A. Fundamental legal principles**

**1. Principle of indemnity**

The insurer should restore the insured to approximately the **same financial position** held before the loss. The insured should not profit. This prevents overinsurance and reduces moral hazard. Loss is measured by:

- **Actual cash value (ACV)** = replacement cost − depreciation (or fair market value, or the broad evidence rule).
- **Replacement cost** insurance: no deduction for depreciation.
- **Valued policy:** a fixed amount is paid for total loss, e.g. antiques or paintings.

*Exceptions:* valued policies, valued policy laws, replacement cost insurance, and **life insurance** (a valued contract, because human life cannot be measured).

**2. Principle of insurable interest**

The insured must stand to **lose financially** if the loss occurs. Its purposes are to prevent gambling, reduce moral hazard and measure the loss. In property insurance it must exist **at the time of loss**. In life insurance it must exist **only at the inception** of the policy. Self, spouse, dependants, creditors and business partners have insurable interest.

**3. Principle of subrogation**

After paying the loss, the insurer takes over the insured's **legal rights to recover** from a negligent third party. Its purposes are to prevent the insured from collecting twice, hold the guilty party responsible, and keep premiums low. It does not apply to life insurance or most personal health insurance.

**4. Principle of utmost good faith (uberrimae fidei)**

Both parties must be completely honest. This principle is supported by three doctrines:

- **Representations:** statements made by the applicant. A *material, false* representation that is *relied upon* makes the contract voidable.
- **Concealment:** intentional failure to disclose a material fact makes the contract voidable.
- **Warranty:** a statement or promise that is part of the contract, such as "a watchman will be on duty". A breach allows the insurer to deny the claim.

**5. Principle of proximate cause**

The insurer is liable only if the **dominant, effective cause** (causa proxima) of the loss is an insured peril. Example: fire caused by lightning is covered, while fire caused by war, if war is excluded, is not.

**6. Principle of contribution**

When the same property is insured with **more than one insurer**, the insurers share the loss in proportion to their coverage (pro-rata liability). The insured cannot recover more than the actual loss. This is a corollary of indemnity.

**7. Principle of loss minimisation**

The insured must take reasonable steps to minimise the loss, acting as an uninsured prudent person would.

**B. Requirements of a valid insurance contract**

1. **Offer and acceptance:** usually, the proposal by the applicant is the offer, and the issue of the policy by the insurer is the acceptance.
2. **Consideration:** the premium and the promise to follow policy conditions.
3. **Competent parties:** the parties must be of legal age and sound mind, and the insurer must be licensed (by the NIA in Nepal).
4. **Legal purpose:** contracts that encourage illegal acts are void.

**C. Distinct legal characteristics**

| Characteristic | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Aleatory | Values exchanged are unequal and depend on chance |
| Unilateral | Only the insurer makes a legally enforceable promise |
| Conditional | The insurer must pay only if the insured meets the policy conditions |
| Personal | Property insurance insures the person, not the property; assignment needs consent |
| Contract of adhesion | The insurer drafts the terms; ambiguities are interpreted in favour of the insured |

**Conclusion:** These principles keep insurance fair, prevent gambling and fraud, and preserve its basic purpose of indemnity.

### Q20. Explain about agriculture insurance. Why do you think agriculture insurance is important for countries like Nepal?

**Solution:**

**Concept of agriculture insurance**

Agriculture insurance protects farmers against **financial losses to crops, livestock, poultry and fishery** caused by natural perils, diseases and other uncontrollable events. It compensates them so they can continue farming.

**Types of agriculture insurance**

1. **Crop insurance:** covers paddy, maize, wheat, vegetables, fruits, tea, coffee, etc. against drought, flood, hailstorm, landslide, pest and disease.
2. **Livestock insurance:** covers cattle, buffalo, goats, pigs, etc. against death due to disease or accident.
3. **Poultry insurance:** covers birds against death due to disease (e.g. bird flu) or accident.
4. **Fishery insurance:** covers fish ponds against disease, flood and pollution.
5. **Others:** bee-keeping, greenhouse/tunnel farming and plantation insurance.

**Approaches**

- **Indemnity-based insurance:** compensation is based on the actual assessed loss of the farm. It is accurate but has high assessment costs and moral hazard.
- **Index-based (parametric) insurance:** payment is triggered by an index such as rainfall, temperature or area yield. There is no farm-level loss assessment, settlement is fast and costs are low, but there is *basis risk* (the farm loss may not match the index).

**Agriculture insurance in Nepal**

- It started under the Agriculture Insurance Directive (2069 B.S.) issued by Beema Samiti, now the **Nepal Insurance Authority (NIA)**.
- The government provides a **premium subsidy of approximately 80%** (as per the directive). The farmer pays only about 20%.
- Non-life insurers are mandated to provide agriculture and livestock insurance. Reinsurance support comes from **Nepal Re-Insurance Company**.

**Importance of agriculture insurance for Nepal**

1. **Dependence on agriculture:** over 60% of the population depends on agriculture for a living, and it contributes about a quarter of GDP. Protecting farmers protects the economy.
2. **High exposure to climate and natural risks:** monsoon floods, landslides, drought, hailstorms, cold waves and climate change cause frequent and severe crop and livestock losses.
3. **Income stability and poverty reduction:** compensation prevents farmers from falling into debt traps and distress sales of assets.
4. **Access to credit:** insured crops and livestock act as collateral. Banks and cooperatives lend more readily, which supports agricultural financing.
5. **Encourages commercialisation and modern technology:** farmers can take the risk of investing in high-value crops, improved breeds and commercial farms.
6. **Food security:** quick recovery after losses maintains production and national food supply.
7. **Reduces the government's disaster relief burden:** a planned risk transfer replaces ad hoc relief.
8. **Reduces migration:** more stable farm income reduces youth migration abroad.
9. **Rural development and financial inclusion:** brings rural farmers into the formal financial system.

**Challenges in Nepal:** low awareness, small and fragmented landholdings, difficult loss assessment, moral hazard and fraud, delays in claim settlement, limited data for index insurance, and low insurer interest because of high costs.

**Way forward:** awareness programmes, index-based products, digital claim processes, timely release of subsidy and cooperative-based distribution.

**Conclusion:** For an agrarian, disaster-prone country like Nepal, agriculture insurance is a vital tool for protecting farmers' livelihoods, ensuring food security and promoting agricultural growth.

### Q21. Insurance is one of the heavily regulated area of the overall financial system. In light of this statement, explain the reasons for insurance regulations.

**Solution:**

**Introduction**

Insurance is regulated heavily because insurers collect premiums in advance and promise to pay large sums far into the future. Contracts are complex, and the public depends on insurers to remain solvent. Failure of an insurer harms policyholders and the economy. In Nepal, insurance is regulated by the **Nepal Insurance Authority (NIA)**, formerly Beema Samiti, under the **Insurance Act, 2079**.

**Reasons for insurance regulation**

**1. Maintain insurer solvency**

This is the most important reason. Premiums are paid in advance, but claims may be paid many years later, especially in life insurance. Regulation ensures that insurers have enough capital, reserves and solvency margins, and sound investments, so they can pay claims. Insolvency would cause great hardship to policyholders.

**2. Compensate for inadequate consumer knowledge**

Insurance contracts are technical and complex. Most consumers cannot judge policy terms, prices or the financial strength of insurers. Regulators approve policy forms and provide information to protect consumers.

**3. Ensure reasonable rates**

Rates should be **adequate** (to keep insurers solvent), **not excessive** (to avoid overcharging) and **not unfairly discriminatory**. In Nepal, some rates are tariff-based, e.g. motor third-party and agriculture.

**4. Make insurance available**

Regulation ensures availability of essential coverages to all, including high-risk or underserved segments. Examples are compulsory third-party motor cover, subsidised agriculture and livestock insurance, micro-insurance, and the requirement to open branches in rural areas.

**5. Prevent unfair practices and protect policyholders**

Regulation prevents misleading advertising, twisting, rebating, misrepresentation by agents and unfair claim practices. It also handles consumer complaints and ensures fair and prompt claim settlement.

**6. Insurance is affected with a public interest**

Insurance supports credit, trade and families' financial security, and it mobilises long-term savings for investment. Its failure has wide social and economic consequences, and it is a source of potential systemic risk.

**Methods of regulation**

- **Legislation:** acts passed by parliament, e.g. the Insurance Act, 2079.
- **Courts:** decisions interpreting contracts and the constitutionality of laws.
- **Regulatory authority:** a government agency, the **NIA**, issues regulations, directives and circulars, licenses insurers and agents, and supervises them.

**Areas regulated**

| Area | What is regulated |
|---|---|
| Formation & licensing | Minimum paid-up capital, licence of insurers, brokers, agents and surveyors |
| Solvency | Capital adequacy, solvency margin, reserves, investment directives, reinsurance (e.g. cession to Nepal Re) |
| Rates | Rate approval, tariffs |
| Policy forms | Approval of new products and policy wording |
| Sales practices & consumer protection | Agent licensing and conduct, commission limits, grievance handling |
| Claims | Timely settlement, surveyor appointment |
| Financial reporting | Audited statements, actuarial valuation, NFRS compliance |
| Taxation | Premium tax, income tax on insurers |
| Liquidation | Rehabilitation and winding-up of weak insurers |

**Conclusion:** Insurance is heavily regulated because it is a long-term promise of future payment and is affected with a strong public interest. Regulation protects policyholders, keeps insurers solvent and maintains public confidence in the financial system.

## Group D — Comprehensive / Case Study (4 × 5 = 20)

### Q22. Read the following case carefully and answer the question that follow:

Aarav Sharma is a 42 years old Senior Project Manager and the primary breadwinner for his family with a spouse and two children. He is also a stock investor and trader. Recently, he decided to quit his job and start his own venture named Everest Tech Solutions, a consultancy firm that specializes in digital transformation for local financial institutions. He has invested a significant portion of his personal savings into the startup, leased a modern office space, and hired five employees. As Everest Tech Solutions begins its operations, Aarav faces a complex web of risks that threaten both his personal financial stability and the viability of his new business.

Aarav's family relies entirely on his income. If he were to suffer a premature death, become permanently disabled or get diagnosed with a critical illness, his family would be unable to meet their living expenses and education costs for their children. Further, his family will not have funds to pay off the mortgage on their house and the car. The new office contains expensive server equipment and high-end laptops. A fire or a localized earthquake could result in a total loss of physical assets. There is also the risk of data breach on the client's server due to a configuration error his employee. The loss of sensitive customer data and business interruption could expose his business to enormous liability.

Aarav is planning to purchase a life insurance policy to ensures that in the event of his untimely demise, his family receives a lumpsum to clear the mortgage and fund his children's education. He is also worried that if he becomes permanently disabled due to accident or gets diagnosed with any critical illness, he will not be able to work and his regular income would be lost.

With years of experience, Aarav also knows there are insurance coverages available to cover the office and its contents (servers, furniture, and electronics) against fire, lightning, and natural disasters like earthquakes or floods. He is also aware about the professional liability insurance that can protect his company against data breaches or other losses due to negligence, errors and omission of his employees during their professional services. He believes the Professional Liability Insurance Policy will provide coverage for the damage to the client as well as the legal costs to be paid for defending his organization. Aarav is also planning to take a group health insurance policy for his five employees to retain the employees as well as to ensure that they have access to quality healthcare without financial strain.

Case Study Questions

a) Identify all the pure risks and speculative risks faced by Aarav or his organization.

b) Which type of life insurance policy do you suggest to Aarav? Justify your answer. What additional benefits could be added to the policy to enhance the coverage?

c) How can a Professional Liability Insurance protect Everest Tech Solutions?

d) Why do you think is the decision to provide Group Health Insurance for the staffs considered a risk management tool, rather than just an additional expense to the organization?

**Solution:**

#### (a) Pure risks and speculative risks faced by Aarav / Everest Tech Solutions

A **pure risk** has only two possible outcomes: loss or no loss. It is generally insurable. A **speculative risk** has three possible outcomes: loss, gain or no change. It is generally not insurable.

**Pure risks**

| Category | Pure risk identified in the case |
|---|---|
| Personal risks | Premature death of Aarav (family loses all income; mortgage, car loan and education unfunded) |
| | Permanent disability due to accident (loss of earning capacity) |
| | Critical illness (heavy medical cost + loss of income) |
| | Illness/injury of the five employees (medical costs, absenteeism) |
| | Loss of key person (Aarav himself, the business depends on him) |
| Property risks | Fire, lightning damaging servers, laptops, furniture, office contents |
| | Earthquake or flood causing total loss of physical assets |
| | Theft or burglary of equipment |
| | **Business interruption** – loss of income and extra expenses after a property loss |
| Liability risks | **Data breach** on the client's server due to an employee's configuration error |
| | Professional negligence, errors & omissions in consultancy services |
| | Loss of sensitive customer data → lawsuits, regulatory penalties |
| | Employer's liability for workplace injury of employees |
| | Liability arising from the leased premises and from his car (third-party motor liability) |

**Speculative risks**

| Speculative risk | Why speculative |
|---|---|
| Quitting a secure job to start Everest Tech Solutions | The venture may succeed (profit), fail (loss) or break even |
| Investing personal savings in the startup | Return may be high, nil or negative |
| Stock investing and trading | Share prices can rise or fall |
| Leasing a modern office | The cost is fixed but the benefit depends on business growth |
| Hiring five employees | Payroll may or may not generate enough revenue |
| Market/competition risk in digital transformation for financial institutions | Demand, competition and technology changes |
| Taking a mortgage and car loan (financial leverage) and interest-rate changes | Asset value or rates may move favourably or unfavourably |

**Conclusion:** Aarav can transfer the pure risks through life, health, property, business interruption and liability insurance. The speculative risks must be managed through business strategy, diversification and hedging.

#### (b) Recommended life insurance policy and riders

**Suggested policy: Term life insurance**, preferably a **level term plan up to age 60–65** with a high sum assured. Part of the cover may be **decreasing term (mortgage protection / reducing term)** to match the outstanding home and car loan balance.

**Justification**

1. **High need, limited cash:** Aarav is 42, his family depends entirely on him, and he has a mortgage, a car loan and two children's education to fund. He needs a **large lump sum**. Term insurance gives the **highest coverage at the lowest premium**.
2. **Business cash flow is tight:** most of his savings are invested in the startup. Expensive endowment or whole life premiums would strain liquidity. Under the "buy term and invest the difference" approach, savings can go into the business or investments.
3. **Temporary need:** the need is greatest *now*, during the next 15–20 years until the loan is repaid and the children are independent. Term cover matches this period exactly.
4. **Needs approach:** cover should equal the outstanding mortgage + car loan + children's education fund + income replacement for the family for several years + final expenses − existing assets. Only term insurance can affordably provide such a sum.
5. **Mortgage protection:** a decreasing term policy assigned to the bank ensures the house is not lost on his death.
6. **Later, once the business stabilises,** he may add a whole life or endowment policy for savings and estate purposes, and claim the tax deduction (up to Rs 40,000 premium).

**Riders to enhance coverage**

- **Accidental Death Benefit (ADB):** an extra sum assured if death is due to an accident.
- **Permanent Total/Partial Disability (PTD/PPD) benefit:** pays a lump sum or income if he is disabled by an accident. This directly addresses his worry about losing income.
- **Critical Illness rider:** a lump sum on diagnosis of cancer, heart attack, stroke, kidney failure, etc.
- **Waiver of Premium rider:** future premiums are waived if he becomes disabled or critically ill, so the policy continues.
- **Income/Family Benefit rider:** a monthly income to the family after his death, in addition to the lump sum.
- **Hospital cash / medical rider:** a daily allowance during hospitalisation.
- **Spouse or child term rider:** cover for other family members.

#### (c) How Professional Liability Insurance protects Everest Tech Solutions

**Meaning:** Professional Liability Insurance, also called **Errors & Omissions (E&O)** insurance, protects professionals and firms against **legal liability arising from negligence, errors, omissions or inadequate work** in providing professional services, which cause financial loss to clients. For an IT consultancy, it is often combined with **cyber liability** insurance.

**How it protects the firm**

1. **Covers damages to clients:** if an employee's configuration error causes a data breach or system failure at a client financial institution, the policy pays the **compensation, settlement or court judgment** awarded to the client.
2. **Legal defence costs:** it pays lawyers' fees, investigation and court costs, even if the claim is groundless. These costs can be huge for a startup.
3. **Data breach and cyber liability (if included or endorsed):** covers notification costs, forensic investigation, data restoration, regulatory fines (where insurable) and third-party claims for loss of sensitive customer data.
4. **Business interruption / network interruption losses** (under cyber extensions).
5. **Protects the firm's capital and survival:** a single large claim could bankrupt a new firm with limited capital and Aarav's personal savings. Transferring this low-frequency, high-severity risk is the correct risk-management response.
6. **Enhances credibility:** banks and financial institutions often *require* their IT vendors to carry E&O and cyber cover before awarding contracts. The policy therefore also helps win business.
7. **Covers employees' acts:** the firm is vicariously liable for its five employees' mistakes, and these are covered.

**Key policy features to note**

- Usually written on a **claims-made basis**: the claim must be made during the policy period.
- **Retroactive date:** errors committed after this date are covered.
- **Limits of liability** per claim and in aggregate, plus a **deductible**.
- **Exclusions:** intentional or fraudulent acts, bodily injury or property damage (covered under general liability), contractual penalties, and prior known claims.

**Conclusion:** With adequate limits, this policy transfers Everest Tech's most dangerous liability exposure to an insurer. It protects both the client relationship and the firm's survival.

#### (d) Group Health Insurance as a risk management tool rather than an expense

Group health insurance covers all five employees under one master policy for hospitalisation and medical expenses. It is a **risk management tool** because it reduces or transfers several loss exposures of the firm:

1. **Transfer of employee health risk:** the uncertain cost of employees' illness or injury (which the employer might otherwise feel obliged to pay) is converted into a small, certain, budgeted premium.
2. **Reduces human resource / key-person loss exposure:** in a five-person knowledge firm, each employee is critical. Health cover improves **retention** and reduces **turnover**, along with the costs of losing trained staff, recruiting, retraining and delaying client projects.
3. **Loss reduction:** employees seek **early treatment** without financial worry. This reduces the severity of illness and the number of days absent.
4. **Higher productivity and morale:** employees who feel financially secure are more focused and motivated. This also indirectly lowers the risk of errors that could cause professional liability claims.
5. **Business continuity:** healthy staff and quick recovery mean projects are delivered on time and business interruption is avoided.
6. **Cost efficiency through group underwriting:** group policies have **lower per-head premiums**, usually no individual medical examination, and cover for pre-existing conditions. Adverse selection is controlled because employees join for employment, not for insurance.
7. **Tax advantage:** the premium is a **deductible business expense**, so the net cost is lower.
8. **Legal and social compliance:** it helps meet the employer's obligations toward employee welfare under the Labour Act and Social Security Fund medical schemes. It also demonstrates social responsibility, which is a post-loss objective of risk management.
9. **Attracts talent:** it gives a startup a competitive advantage in hiring skilled IT professionals.

**Conclusion:** The premium is not a mere cost. It is an **investment that protects the firm's most valuable asset, its people**, and stabilises earnings. This is why group health insurance is a deliberate risk management decision for Everest Tech Solutions.
